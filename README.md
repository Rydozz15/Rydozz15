# Juan Pablo Fuentes-Abarca (Jotapé)

MSc candidate in Computer Science at PUC Chile, with a first degree in Psychology. I study how to design, evaluate, and audit LLM-based systems that interact with people, with AI in Education as my main application domain.

I came to this from a practical problem (scaling academic mentoring with AI) and ended up asking a different question: who gets to contest an AI's judgment, and what do we lose when we trust it by default?

## Research interests

- **Human-AI interaction, collaboration, and augmentation:** how people and LLMs actually work together, and what stays "yours" in that exchange.
- **Evaluation and auditing of LLM systems:** including the limits of LLM-as-a-Judge and how to redesign evaluation protocols with humans in the loop.
- **Contestability, over-reliance, and responsible AI:** designing systems whose behavior users can question, adjust, and push back on.
- **AI in Education:** academic mentoring, tutoring, and learning tools, studied with methods from psychology and HCI.

## Selected work

**Designing Conversational Friction: A Contestable System Prompt for AI-Assisted Mentoring of Women in Latin American Academia**
Accepted for presentation at Conversations 2026. [Code and materials](https://github.com/Rydozz15/conversations2026)

**From Broken Instrument to Redesigned Protocol: Auditing and Sociotechnical Redesign of an LLM-as-a-Judge Pipeline**
Poster, RISE AI Latin America Summit 2026 (Education Track). Started as a failed attempt to replace an expert panel with LLM judges, and became a study of why that failed.

**MSc thesis: RAG-based academic mentoring**
Evaluation of mentoring responses from a disciplinary expert vs. a generic LLM vs. a RAG system, part of FONDECYT Project 1241537. Supervisors: Isabel Hilliger and Jorge Baier.

**[ISDDE 2026 Workshop Platform](https://github.com/Rydozz15/workshop-platform)**
Full-stack platform for running AI-driven, case-based workshops and testing system prompts under controlled conditions (A/B designs, informed consent, session analytics, CSV/JSON export for research). Built with Next.js, TypeScript, FastAPI, and Supabase.
